Scientists urge food system overhaul to curb global land degradation

Sub Heading

By Rajat Sharma | August 13, 2025 | 8 min read

Agricultural practices need significant reform to prevent. Photo: Media India Group

The research, conducted by an international team of scientists across 45 countries, represents the most extensive analysis to date of how food production impacts soil health, biodiversity, and ecosystem services.

Critical findings

According to the study, intensive farming practices have led to severe soil erosion, with topsoil being lost at rates 10 to 100 times faster than it can naturally regenerate. Additionally, agricultural expansion continues to be the primary driver of deforestation globally, accounting for approximately 80% of forest loss.

"What we're seeing is a vicious cycle," explains Dr. Amrita Patel, lead author of the study and senior researcher at the Indian Institute of Sustainable Agriculture. "As land degrades, farmers often respond by clearing more forest for new agricultural land or intensifying chemical inputs, which further exacerbates environmental problems."

The study highlights that India faces particularly severe challenges, with over 30% of its land already classified as degraded and an additional 1.5 million hectares being affected annually.

Recommendations for change

The researchers propose a three-pronged approach to transform food systems:

1. Diversification of crops and farming systems

Moving away from monoculture farming toward polyculture systems that incorporate multiple crops, agroforestry, and integrated crop-livestock systems can significantly improve soil health while maintaining productivity.

2. Adoption of regenerative practices

Techniques such as no-till farming, cover cropping, and reduced chemical inputs can help restore soil organic matter and biodiversity while sequestering carbon.

3. Policy reforms and market incentives

Governments need to redirect agricultural subsidies away from practices that degrade land toward those that restore it. Additionally, creating market incentives for sustainable products can help drive change at scale.

Sustainable farming practices can help restore degraded land. Photo: Media India Group

Indian context

In India, where agriculture employs nearly half the workforce and uses 60% of land area, the implications of the study are particularly significant. The country has already begun implementing programs like the National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture, but experts suggest more ambitious action is needed.

"India has an opportunity to become a global leader in sustainable agriculture," says Dr. Rajiv Sharma, Director of the Centre for Science and Environment in New Delhi, who was not involved in the study. "Traditional knowledge combined with modern science can create farming systems that are both productive and regenerative."

The study comes as India faces increasing challenges from climate change, with erratic monsoons and rising temperatures putting additional pressure on agricultural systems.
